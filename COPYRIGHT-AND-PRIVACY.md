# Copyright, Privacy and Source Handling

This is a **public GitHub repository**. Useful does not automatically mean safe or lawful to republish.

## Copyright rule

Do not upload full copies of third-party books, journal articles, newsletters, training manuals, course notes or PDFs merely because a copy can be found in Gmail, Google Drive or somewhere on the web.

Prefer:

1. the rights-holder/publisher page;
2. PubMed/PubMed Central or another legitimate open-access repository;
3. an official downloadable PDF where the owner clearly makes it public;
4. a short original summary plus citation/link.

A PDF being technically downloadable does **not** establish redistribution rights.

## Material that can normally be stored locally

- original notes written for this repository;
- original worksheets/checklists;
- public-domain material;
- Creative Commons material where the specific licence permits redistribution and attribution requirements are followed;
- third-party files where explicit redistribution permission has been obtained.

Record the licence/source next to any mirrored third-party file.

## Private-source rule

Connected Gmail/Drive can be searched to discover useful material, but raw private material should not automatically become public GitHub content.

Do not publish:

- private email threads;
- private email addresses or phone numbers;
- home addresses;
- contact/guest lists;
- third-party personal information;
- private treatment/medical records;
- appointment details;
- login/account information;
- documents containing unrelated confidential material.

When private material points to a useful concept, look for a legitimate public source and link that instead.

## Health privacy

Personal reflection worksheets should be blank templates by default. Completed therapy/session notes should **not** be committed to this public repository unless the repository owner has deliberately decided to make that information public.

Recommended pattern:

- keep blank templates in GitHub;
- keep completed reflections somewhere private;
- only add anonymised/general lessons to the public resource base where useful.

## Claims about practitioners

Only publish qualifications, services, addresses or professional affiliations that are clearly available from a current public professional source. Do not infer credentials from private emails or social connections.

If a private arrangement is not publicly advertised, describe the general learning rather than publicising the arrangement.

## Evidence wording

Distinguish:

- **study finding** — what a particular study observed;
- **systematic-review conclusion** — what a review says about the body of evidence;
- **model/practitioner claim** — what an organisation says about its own approach;
- **participant experience** — meaningful, but not proof of universal efficacy;
- **hypothesis/metaphor** — potentially useful without being established fact.

This is especially important in equine-assisted work, where evocative phrases such as “the horse mirrors you” can be therapeutically useful metaphors but are often presented with more scientific certainty than the evidence supports.

The horse is not a Rorschach test.

A horse moving away can be therapeutically interesting because of your reaction, without pretending we know exactly why the horse moved.

Core learning

Assertiveness without domination

Disappear

Passive

Vague request, apologising for taking space, giving up quickly to avoid discomfort.

Stay present

Assertive

Clear request, proportionate energy, time for a response, respect for both sets of boundaries.

Over-control

Forceful

Escalating pressure mainly because uncertainty or non-compliance feels intolerable.

Transfer to real life

Progress matters outside the arena

  • Recognise activation earlier
  • Ask directly for reasonable things
  • Say no without exploding or over-explaining
  • Tolerate another person’s disappointment
  • Stay present through relational tension

2025 systematic review

31 eligible studies

Positive signals across PTSD, anxiety and mood outcomes, alongside few strong randomised trials and substantial methodological inconsistency.

2015 anxiety study

Project Stride

A tiny randomised pilot combined equine activities with CBT for young women with social anxiety. Interesting result; far too small to settle efficacy.

PubMed ↗
2024 horse welfare review

28 welfare papers

Therapy-horse stress findings were inconsistent, reinforcing the need to actively monitor welfare rather than assume horses enjoy—or dislike—the work.

Useful scepticism

Don’t turn metaphor into fact

Claims such as “the horse always mirrors your emotions” can be meaningful metaphors, but should not be treated as proven diagnostic mechanisms.

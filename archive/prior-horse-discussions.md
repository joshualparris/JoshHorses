## 30 April 2026 — D&D wagon-horse husbandry

A previous discussion involved a fictional D&D wagon team and aimed to make the horse care scientifically plausible. The team included **Sace, Marrow and Vesper** in the Alphie campaign context.

Although the setting was fictional, the practical horse-care guidance given was intentionally real-world grounded:

- do **not** withhold water from a hot/tired horse as an old horsemanship myth;
- provide clean water and allow drinking while cooling, supervised appropriately;
- salt/electrolytes may be relevant after significant sweat, but electrolytes do not replace access to plain water;
- inspect for harness rubs and pressure points;
- check legs, shoes and hooves;
- notice signs that could indicate hoof problems/thrush rather than treating every smell/appearance as normal;
- hay/forage is generally a more appropriate first feed than immediately giving a large grain/oat meal after hard work;
- monitor for discomfort and possible colic signs;
- wagon safety checks discussed included singletrees, doubletree/evener, pole, traces and brake components.

The earlier answer also explicitly avoided claiming that one routine is “100% correct” for every horse. Real care depends on exertion, temperature, health, diet, training, veterinary advice and the specific animal.

### Why retain this?

It is **not therapy evidence**, but it supports a repository-wide principle: horses are animals with their own physiological and welfare needs, not props for a human story or intervention.

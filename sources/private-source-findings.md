**Search date:** 13 September 2026

This file records useful findings from searches across the repository owner’s connected Gmail and Google Drive **without copying private correspondence, contact lists, addresses, phone numbers, health information or other people’s personal data into this public repository**.

Search terms included combinations of:

- horse / horses;
- equine;
- horse therapy / equine therapy;
- equine assisted / equine-assisted;
- hippotherapy;
- Turf the Lot;
- relevant practitioner/business names.

This is a broad keyword audit of the accessible connected accounts, not a claim that every message and file was manually read one-by-one.

## Useful Gmail finding — Bendigo Health

A **March 2026 Bendigo Health Carer Support Services bulletin** advertised an **Equine Assisted Wellbeing** program in Woodend and Mildura. The bulletin described the program as interaction between people and horses intended to promote emotional growth and learning.

This is useful because it is an example of equine-assisted work appearing in material from an Australian public health service, rather than only in commercial practitioner marketing.

A current public Bendigo Health page now describes **Equine Assisted Learning** programs in Woodend in similar terms: safe, unmounted experiences with horses; no previous horse knowledge required; opportunities to explore difficult feelings or simply experience being with horses.

Public current page:
https://www.bendigohealth.org.au/css/events/

The emailed bulletin itself is **not copied into this public repository**.

## Gmail finding — Turf the Lot / older community correspondence

Older correspondence from roughly 2017–2018 contains multiple ordinary community/network references to **Turf the Lot** and people associated with it.

What this establishes:

- Turf the Lot has been part of the owner’s wider community/network history for years.

What it **does not** establish:

- that the older correspondence concerned horse therapy;
- that a particular person had clinical qualifications at that time;
- that Turf the Lot publicly operated a therapy service.

The messages are therefore not reproduced here. They contain other people’s contact details and unrelated correspondence.

## Google Drive finding — nature, health and animal-assisted interventions

Drive contains copies of a report titled **“Townsend Weerasuriya 2010 full report.pdf”**. Searchable text includes discussion of animal-assisted interventions, animal therapy, equine therapy, ecotherapy, adventure therapy and mental wellbeing within a broader nature/health review.

This is an older background resource, not current evidence of efficacy. It is retained in the source audit because it shows that the owner’s archive already contained material connecting animals/nature and mental health.

The report is not mirrored here because provenance/copyright permission for public redistribution has not been established.

## Google Drive finding — equine safety in work-placement guidance

A Drive document titled **“Additional_Information_Parents_Carers-22.DOCX”** includes school/work-placement guidance treating some equine work as potentially high-risk and emphasising risk assessment, suitability of the horse and appropriate approval/supervision.

This is not psychotherapy guidance, but it supports a relevant principle: **horse-based activities require explicit physical-risk management rather than assuming therapeutic intent makes the environment safe**.

The source document is not copied publicly because it belongs to a broader private/school document set.

## Privacy rule used for this audit

This public repository records **what category of useful information was found**, not raw private records.

Do not add:

- private email addresses or phone numbers;
- home addresses;
- third-party names from unrelated private correspondence;
- private health/treatment details;
- guest/contact lists;
- full private emails;
- copyrighted attachments without permission.

If a private source points to something genuinely useful, prefer finding a legitimate public version and linking that instead. Bendigo Health’s current public EAL page is an example of this approach.

## What “horse therapy” can mean

The phrase is used loosely. Different services can be quite different:

- **Equine-assisted psychotherapy (EAP):** psychotherapy delivered by an appropriately qualified mental-health professional while deliberately incorporating horses into treatment.
- **Equine-assisted learning (EAL):** experiential learning/personal-development work with horses; it is not automatically psychotherapy.
- **Therapeutic/adaptive riding:** horsemanship or riding adapted for participant needs; this should not automatically be described as psychotherapy.
- **Hippotherapy:** use of equine movement by appropriately qualified health professionals within disciplines such as physiotherapy, occupational therapy or speech pathology.

Terminology matters because credentials, goals, risks and evidence differ between these services.

## Core themes worth noticing as a participant

Horse work can create a live setting in which to notice:

- what anxiety feels like in the body before it becomes a story in the head;
- breathing, muscle tension, pace, proximity and impulse to approach or withdraw;
- whether a request is clear, hesitant, forceful or apologetic;
- the difference between **assertiveness** and **control**;
- what happens when another living being does not immediately do what you hoped;
- boundaries: your own, the horse’s, and the facilitator’s;
- repair after frustration, uncertainty or disconnection;
- whether an insight from the arena actually transfers into ordinary relationships and decisions.

A useful working question is:

> Can I notice what is happening in me, remain present, communicate clearly without forcing, and stay responsive to the other being’s response?

That is a practical hypothesis to explore, not a claim that horses diagnose emotions or magically “mirror” people.

## Evidence position

The evidence is **promising but not definitive**. A 2025 systematic review covering PTSD, anxiety and mood disorders found that quantitative studies commonly reported at least one positive outcome, but also found few strong randomised studies, inconsistent terminology and considerable methodological heterogeneity. Older reviews have been more critical. This repository therefore separates:

- established psychotherapy principles;
- plausible experiential mechanisms;
- findings supported by studies;
- practitioner/model claims that should not be treated as proven facts.

See [resources/evidence-and-standards.md](resources/evidence-and-standards.md).

## Software and copyright position

Public source code is not automatically free to reuse simply because it is visible on GitHub. The two horse-therapy repositories reviewed for the software explorer did not show repository-level licence files in their inspected roots, and the uploaded static archive did not contain a licence notice in the inspected HTML/CSS/JavaScript.

JoshHorses therefore uses a **clean-room approach**: study architecture and workflows, then implement useful ideas independently with original code, synthetic data and links back to the original projects. Do not copy or publicly redeploy third-party code, text, templates, branding or imagery without a suitable licence or permission.

See [archive/software-and-copyright-review-2026-09-15.md](archive/software-and-copyright-review-2026-09-15.md), [resources/software-patterns.md](resources/software-patterns.md) and [software-lab/](software-lab/).

## Safety and scope

This repository is educational. It is not a substitute for assessment or treatment by a qualified mental-health or medical professional, and it does not tell a participant how to handle an unfamiliar horse without the person supervising the session.

Horse welfare is part of ethical practice. The horse is a sentient participant, not a prop or diagnostic device.

## Contribution rule

Prefer primary research, systematic reviews, professional standards and original publisher/practitioner sources. Do not upload copyrighted books, paid course material, private emails, private health information or third-party PDFs without clear permission. Link to legitimate originals instead.
